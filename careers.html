The brief

A brokerage built
to fix a trust problem.

In 2006 the Canadian mortgage market had a transparency problem, not a rate problem. Rates were competitive — the information around them was not.

The problem we inherited

Buyers arrived at a bank branch with a spreadsheet they didn’t understand, a broker who couldn’t quote a number without checking, and no way to compare lenders against each other. The rate was the smallest part of the decision.

Our founder spent fifteen years watching good families walk away from good homes because the process felt opaque — and quietly built the opposite of it: a brokerage where the numbers are public, the fee is disclosed before anyone signs, and one licensed human owns the file from first call to funding.

Twenty years later that original brief is still the test we hire against. If a candidate can’t explain a rate in plain language, they don’t get the job — no matter how many files they’ve closed.

The journey

Six moments that
made the company.

Every version of Lead.io was an answer to something that went wrong for a client. Here they are, in order.

  1. 2006 Chapter 01

    One desk, one promise

    Nurul Anam opens a single-desk brokerage in Toronto with a rule that still governs the business: quote the number before you ask for the file. The first year, eleven clients. Every one of them referred somebody.

    • Founded
    • Toronto, ON
    • 1 broker
  2. 2010 Chapter 02

    The rate-hold standard

    A client lost a week of shopping to a rate expiry. We institutionalised 120-day rate holds as a default rather than a favour — a practice we still apply on every purchase file today.

    • Rate holds standardised
    • 100th file
  3. 2014 Chapter 03

    Forty lenders, one comparison

    We stopped working with three or four “preferred” banks and built a panel of 40+ lenders we can shop at once. It let us tell a client the truth: their own bank was not offering them the best rate.

    • Lender panel → 40
    • Savings average $8,400
  4. 2018 Chapter 04

    Five languages, one bench

    The INVIS network partnership took us beyond Ontario. We hired deliberately across Toronto, Vancouver and Calgary and built an in-house language bench — English, Spanish, Filipino, Punjabi and Bengali — so a client never had to explain their file twice.

    • BC & AB licensed
    • 5 languages
  5. 2022 Chapter 05

    The 15-minute promise

    We rebuilt intake from scratch: one short form, one licensed broker, a reply inside a business hour. No call centre, no bot, no handoff into a queue. Applications went from days to minutes.

    • Intake rebuilt
    • 1-hour reply SLA
  6. 2026 Chapter 06

    Opening the bench

    Thirty-one people now work across three provinces and a second desk in Dhaka. For the first time we’re hiring not to plug a gap, but to widen the bench — and we’re writing this page so you can read the whole thing before you apply.

    • 31 teammates
    • 12 open roles

Life here

What the job is
actually like.

No ping-pong-table promises. Here’s what we can actually commit to, in writing, and what we ask of you in return.

Real hours, protected

Client calls end by 8pm. No evening/weekend rotations, no “just checking in” at 9pm. We respect the line because we’ve lived on the other side of it.

A path, not a ceiling

Every broker here can move into senior brokerage, team lead, lender relations or operations. We map it in your first 90 days and review it every six months.

Tools that keep up

CRM, lender portal, e-signature and document automation — licensed, maintained, and yours to use on day one. No personal laptop, no out-of-pocket software.

Five languages, one team

English, Spanish, Filipino, Punjabi and Bengali — spoken daily, not listed on a website. Our bench in Dhaka supports clients across three time zones.

Learning budget

Every teammate gets an annual budget and five dedicated days for licensing, designations and certifications. We cover the exam fees.

Paid from day one

Health, dental and vision from your first day, plus a matched RRSP or TFSA contribution. Full transparency, no waiting period games.

How we decide

Three rules we
hire and fire on.

These aren’t poster words. They’re the tiebreakers in every interview panel, every promotion review and every performance conversation we run.

  1. 01

    Say the true number

    If the honest rate is worse than what the client expects, we say so — with the reasoning. Padding a file to look good costs us the client and the referral.

  2. 02

    One human owns the file

    No transfers between departments, no ticket numbers, no “I’ve been put on hold.” A licensed broker takes your application and closes it. That person answers the phone.

  3. 03

    Plain language wins

    If a client can’t repeat the terms back to us in their own words, we haven’t explained them. Complexity is our problem to solve, not theirs.
